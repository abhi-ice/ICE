What makes this genuinely different from v5

  • Two columns, not one. Far-left 54px rail = one icon per section. Right 230px content column = items for the active section ONLY. The other ~28 items don't exist on screen until you ask for them.
  • Section identity is spatial. Color is reinforced in 3 places at once — the rail-icon highlight, the rail's slim accent stripe, and the content column's section tag. Your eye literally follows the same color from rail → column → header.
  • Hover tooltips on the rail. While the icon-only rail is dense, hovering shows the label — like VSCode and Slack do. Power users learn the icons in a week and stop reading tooltips.
  • Per-section filter input at the top of each content column. Type "delay" inside Daily Ops → only Delays remains. Different from a global ⌘K because it's scoped.
  • Notification dots on the rail (the orange pip on Daily Ops + Procurement) — same as Discord/Slack unread indicators. Signal "this section has pending stuff" without opening it.
  • ⌘K still at the bottom of the rail for global navigation. Avatar at the very bottom (always reachable).
  • Visual rhythm changes. v5 was a long flat list with section dividers. v5b feels like a desktop app (think iCal, Mac Mail, VSCode) — pick a "module," then work inside it.
  • Mobile-friendly base. The 54px rail can collapse to a horizontal bottom bar on mobile; the content column slides over the page. Pattern translates 1:1 to phone.

Trade-offs (honest)

  • One extra click to change sections (vs. v5 where everything's already visible).
  • Slightly more horizontal real estate (54 + 230 = 284px vs. v5's 256px). +28px.
  • Users have to learn the rail icons. Tooltips help but the first day feels less obvious.
