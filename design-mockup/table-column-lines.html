Honest take from looking at modern B2B SaaS in 2026

  • Almost nobody uses option A (full grid lines) anymore. Procore, Autodesk Construction Cloud, PlanGrid all dropped it 2-3 years ago. The "Excel look" reads as old-school enterprise.
  • Linear, Notion, Stripe, Vercel, Airtable, Asana all use the current pattern (no vertical lines, only horizontal row dividers). The reason: with ~10+ columns and 50+ rows, vertical lines create visual clutter and slow row-scan.
  • C (header-only) is the modern compromise — implies columns without enforcing them. Used by Linear's issue lists and Vercel's deployment tables.
  • D (alternating column tint) is the Notion DB / Airtable approach — best when columns have very different content widths (a short number + a long description).
  • SiteOps has 10-15 column tables (Tasks, Lookahead, Submittals, Activity Log). Option A would make them feel cramped and old. Option C is the upgrade that adds "table feel" without the heaviness.

My recommendation

  • Stay on current for the 10+ column workhorse tables (Tasks, Lookahead, Submittals, DR Activity Log, Resource Leveling) — they're scanning surfaces where horizontal lines are enough.
  • Switch to C (header-only) for the small-column tables (Projects, Vendors, RFIs list, Delays list, Photos AI stats — 4-7 columns). Adds the "structured" feel without choking the layout.
  • Skip A and D — A is dated, D is gimmicky for our column counts.
